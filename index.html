Enterprise CCTV Platform Migration

CCTV / VMSRisk & SchedulingAvigilon
Situation
A live environment running roughly 1,330 cameras was reaching end-of-life on its recording infrastructure, with zero tolerance for gaps in the footage — not a system that could go dark for a maintenance window.
My role
Owned this as a project — building the phased schedule, coordinating multiple vendors, and making the go/no-go call at each milestone before the next phase started.
Approach
Sequenced the replacement into phases rather than one cutover, treating the parallel-run window as the project's key risk control — old and new infrastructure ran side by side over 10 Gbps fibre with VLAN re-routing until the new side had proven itself under real load, keeping a safe rollback point built into the schedule at every stage.
Outcome
Completed the full migration with no gap in recording continuity and no unplanned downtime.
Lesson learned: the parallel-operation window cost more time upfront than a straight cutover would have — but it's the only way I'd sign off on changing live security infrastructure with this little margin for error.

Multi-Site Security Programme, 15+ Sites

Access ControlProgramme MgmtTeam Leadership
Situation
A portfolio of 15+ sites and 5,000+ devices across CCTV/VMS, access control, intercom, barriers and key management had no single delivery standard — quality depended on which team happened to be on site that week.
My role
Directed 30+ engineers and technicians across the portfolio, owning resourcing and workload allocation across sites, and served as the escalation point once an issue moved past routine troubleshooting.
Approach
Standardised design review, commissioning and handover documentation across every site, applied the same structured fault-isolation process to every incident instead of letting each site improvise its own, and managed vendor and supplier engagement for pricing, technical compliance and support across the portfolio.
Outcome
Maintained a project and maintenance portfolio valued above AED 5M without a separate dedicated QA function.
Lesson learned: the documentation standard ended up mattering more than any single tool choice — it's what let a new technician get productive fast, and kept one site's incident from turning into a portfolio-wide scramble.

Automated Vehicle Access Integration

ANPRBarriersSystems Integration
Situation
Vehicle access relied on a manual, guard-operated process with no clean audit trail.
My role
Designed and troubleshot the integration between ANPR cameras, barriers, loop detectors and the underlying safety-loop/relay logic.
Approach
Built and tested the failure paths — a false plate read, a power interruption — before go-live, not just the path where everything works.
Outcome
Automated, logged vehicle access with a defined, tested fallback for edge cases.
Lesson learned: most of the real engineering effort went into what happens when something goes wrong, not what happens when everything works — that's usually where a project like this actually fails if you skip it.
